# Indigenous Peoples Games

The Indigenous Peoples Games is a Philippine Sports Commission programme that organises traditional indigenous sports and games in indigenous communities around the country. It was launched in 2018 with the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples and the National Commission for Culture and the Arts.

---

## What the programme does

The PSC runs the Indigenous Peoples Games to preserve and promote the cultural heritage and traditions carried in indigenous sport. Instead of bringing mainstream sports to indigenous communities, the programme stages the games those communities already play, in their own areas.

It is run in cooperation with two partner agencies:

- **National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)**
- **National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA)**

|                        |                                                                           |

| **Where**              | Tagum City, Davao del Norte                                               |
| **When**               | 27 to 28 April 2018                                                       |
| **Participants**       | 300 indigenous people                                                     |
| **Tribes represented** | Ata-Manobo, Dibabawon, Mandaya, Mangguangan, Mansaka and Sama             |
| **Led by**             | PSC Indigenous Peoples Games oversight Commissioner Charles Raymond Maxey |
| **Supported by**       | Province of Davao del Norte and the NCIP                                  |

The programme has continued in other provinces since. The PSC does not publish a list of the legs held after 2018.

---

## Legislative follow-through

Through the PSC's initiative, the House of Representatives approved **House Bill No. 8626, the Philippine Indigenous Games Preservation Act of 2017**, in December 2018. The bill was authored by Representative Salvador Belaro of the 1-Ang Edukasyon Party-list, who described traditional games as the "best avenue for self-expression and promoting peace, harmony, goodwill, and camaraderie among the indigenous peoples."

This was approval by the House of Representatives. The PSC does not publish anything further on whether the measure became law, so do not treat it as an enacted statute without checking with Congress.
