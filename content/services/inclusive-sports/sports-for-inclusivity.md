# Sports for inclusivity, heritage, and peace

Several Philippine Sports Commission programmes are built for communities that mainstream competition tends to leave out: indigenous peoples, people with disabilities, women and girls, out-of-school youth, and communities affected by conflict. This page brings them together and explains the mandate behind them.

## The mandate behind this work

Republic Act No. 6847 makes inclusion a statutory objective rather than an optional extra.

- **Section 6(a)**: the PSC provides the leadership, formulates the policies and sets the direction of national amateur sports promotion and development, "particularly giving emphasis on grass-roots participation".
- **Section 6(b)**: the PSC encourages wide participation of **all sectors**, government and private, in amateur sports promotion and development.
- **Section 7(m)**: the PSC encourages and sustains the creation of regional, provincial, municipal and barangay or school district sports promotion and development councils, which initiate and coordinate sports activities in their own areas.

The PSC's own mission statement commits it to "creating equitable opportunities for participation in sports by all sectors" and to "providing assistance to stakeholders and partners".

## Sport for peace in Mindanao

The PSC has taken its grassroots and sports-for-peace work into Mindanao directly. In an undated account on its own site, the PSC describes a chairman's tour in the third week of January covering six cities across the region, Davao City, Cotabato City, Marawi City, Iligan City, and Ozamis and Tangub City in Misamis Occidental, to expand the agency's reach in grassroots development and sports-for-peace initiatives. The PSC does not state the year, and the chairman who made the trip is no longer in office.

The [Mindanao Peace Games](/services/grassroots-sports/mindanao-peace-games) is the clearest example of the approach in practice: a university-convened festival, using competition between schools across the region to build relationships rather than rivalries, which the PSC backed once the organisers asked.

## How to bring an inclusive sports programme to your community

**The PSC publishes no single application route, form, or schedule for any of these programmes.** The realistic approach is to write to the PSC through the office closest to your request, and to work with the national partner agency for your community.

| If you are                            | Start with                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| An indigenous people's organisation   | The PSC, plus the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples                        |
| A local government wanting para sport | Your own Persons with Disability Affairs Office, plus the PSC                      |
| A school or university                | The PSC, plus your regional sports association                                     |
| A barangay or municipality            | The PSC, and consider setting up a local sports council under RA 6847 Section 7(m) |

| Office                                     | Phone                      | Email                      |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Assistance Coordination Division           | 8525-0808 local 138 or 156 | supportservices@psc.gov.ph |
| Program, Research and Development Division | 8525-0808 local 188 or 186 | prdd@psc.gov.ph            |
