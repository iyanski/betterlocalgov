# Safe sports and athlete protection

The Philippine Sports Commission has begun work on safeguarding by training national sports associations to build their own safeguarding systems. It has not published a safeguarding policy, a code of conduct, or a way for an athlete to report abuse. If you need to report something now, this page tells you where to go and what is missing.

---

## If you need to report abuse or harassment now

**The PSC does not publish a safeguarding reporting channel, a complaints form, a designated safeguarding officer, or a hotline.** That is the honest position, and pretending otherwise would be worse than saying it.

Your realistic routes are:

1. **Your national sports association.** Under the PSC's 2024 workshop, NSAs were asked to build their own safeguarding systems, and discipline over athletes and officials is an NSA function under Section 13(g) of Republic Act No. 6847. Ask your NSA who its safeguarding contact is.
2. **The PSC Legal Affairs Office**, which is the Commission office whose remit is closest. Officer-in-Charge Atty. Neil Emerson B. Samson, 8525-0808 local 160, legalaffairs@psc.gov.ph.
3. **The NSA Affairs Office**, nsaoo@psc.gov.ph, if the concern is about how an NSA is handling a case.
4. **The Philippine National Police or the appropriate court or agency**, if a criminal offence may have been committed. Safeguarding processes do not replace the law.

If a child is involved, treat it as urgent and go to law enforcement as well as the sports bodies.

## The 2024 Safe Sport Workshop

The PSC hosted a **Safe Sport Workshop for National Sports Associations on 19 and 20 April 2024**. It was an initiative of Commissioner Olivia "Bong" Coo, the PSC's oversight commissioner for coaches and athletes welfare.

|                                    |                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who was invited**                | All national sports associations, including sports under the Philippine Paralympic Committee                                       |
| **Who each NSA was asked to send** | A member of its board of trustees or an official, a coach, and an athlete                                                          |
| **Registered**                     | About 40 NSAs                                                                                                                      |
| **Purpose**                        | To help each NSA start developing its own safeguarding system, and to jumpstart safeguarding policy-making inside each association |

The workshop was framed around the International Olympic Committee's commitment to ensuring that every athlete can train and compete in a safe sporting environment, one that is fair, equitable and free from all forms of harassment and abuse.

Commissioner Coo put the aim plainly: "PSC recognised and identified the urgent need to educate the sports community on safeguarding and hopefully jumpstart the policy making for each NSA that attends."

## Where responsibility sits

| Body                             | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **National sports association**  | Decides all questions on the discipline of athletes and officials connected with the association, and all disputes between members, under RA 6847 Section 13(g). Builds and runs its own safeguarding system                        |
| **Philippine Sports Commission** | Holds supervisory and visitorial powers over NSAs for programmes it assists (Section 11(j)), and may impose sanctions on an NSA, team, athlete or sports official for violating its policies, rules and regulations (Section 11(m)) |

The PSC's sanctioning power under Section 11(m) is the statutory lever that could sit behind a safeguarding rule. **The PSC has not published policies, rules or regulations on safeguarding for that power to enforce.**

| An investigation or case handling procedure               | Not published on psc.gov.ph |
| Protection for whistleblowers and reporting athletes      | Not published on psc.gov.ph |
| Which NSAs have completed safeguarding systems since 2024 | Not published on psc.gov.ph |
| Any safe sport activity after April 2024                  | Not published on psc.gov.ph |
