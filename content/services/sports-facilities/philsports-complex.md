# PhilSports Complex (ULTRA)

The PhilSports Complex in Pasig City, originally known as the Philippine Institute of Sports Complex, was built in 1985 as a training base and home for members of the national team. It is one of the Philippine Sports Commission's three main facility properties, and holds four main venues along with the PSC's sports medicine office and an athletes' dining hall.

## What is inside

The PSC names four main facilities on this site: the **PhilSports Multi-Purpose Arena (MPA)**, the **PhilSports Swimming Center**, **PhilSports Football**, and the **Athletics Stadium**.

## History

| Year     | What happened                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1985** | Built in Pasig City, Metro Manila, as the Philippine Institute of Sports Complex, to serve as training facilities and home of the members of the national team.                  |
| **2019** | Renovated for the fourth time when the country hosted the 30th Southeast Asian Games. The Multi-Purpose Arena was the playing venue for the men's indoor volleyball competition. |
| **2020** | Used as one of the COVID-19 medical and quarantine facilities in the country.                                                                                                    |

## Using the complex

Rental rates for PSC venues are published as a downloadable rates document. **The PSC publishes no booking form and no written booking procedure.** See [Book a PSC venue](/services/sports-facilities/book-a-psc-venue).

Under Section 18 of Republic Act No. 6847, no tax of any kind may be levied on the use of sports facilities, whether on the user, the owner or the operator.

---

## What the PSC does not publish

The PSC publishes no venue capacities, court or lane counts, equipment lists, or site map for PhilSports. It also does not explain the origin of the "ULTRA" name, which it uses alongside "PhilSports Complex" without defining it. Those details are left out here rather than guessed at.
