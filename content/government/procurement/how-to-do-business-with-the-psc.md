# How to do business with the PSC

The Philippine Sports Commission buys everything from athlete uniforms and airline tickets to swimming-pool chemicals and stadium repairs, and it buys under Republic Act No. 9184, the Government Procurement Reform Act. This page explains who runs PSC procurement, where opportunities appear, and — honestly — which parts of the process the Commission does not publish.

---

## The Commission's standing invitation

Every PSC bidding page carries the same invitation:

> "Request for Quotations and Invitation to Bid
>
> The Philippine Sports Commission, through its Bids and Awards Committee, invites interested eligible & accredited supplier/manufacturer/service providers to apply for eligibility and to bid as follows:"

| **Officer-in-Charge**  | Caroline S. Tobias, who serves as BAC Secretary on the Commission's organizational chart |
| **Local**              | 143 (through trunkline 8525-0808)                                                        |
| **Email**              | procurement@psc.gov.ph                                                                   |

The Commission also runs a **BAC Secretariat Office**, which appears as an end-user in its own procurement records and has its own documented procedure in the PSC quality management system.

**The Commission publishes no BAC contact details of its own** — no BAC Chairperson, no BAC Secretariat email or phone number, no address for submitting bids and no office hours appear on any of its procurement pages. The Procurement Office above is the published office responsible for the function, and is the address to use if a bid document does not answer your question.

## How a PSC procurement runs

1. **The requirement is planned.** It appears in the Commission's Annual Procurement Plan, filed under the **Transparency Seal** along with indicative, supplemental and updated versions.
2. **The opportunity is published.** A Request for Quotation or Invitation to Bid is posted on the Commission's bidding page for the current year, with the approved budget, the publication date and the closing date. See **Current bid opportunities**.
3. **Bid documents are issued.** The Philippine Bidding Documents, Bid Data Sheet and Terms of Reference are published separately, and any change to them is issued as a Supplemental Bid Bulletin. See **Bid documents and bulletins**.
4. **A pre-bid conference may be held**, with a Notice of Pre-Bid Conference and Opening of Bids, and minutes published afterwards.
5. **Bids are opened**, and the Opening of Bids Minutes are published. Where the schedule changes, the Commission issues a Notice of Postponement and later a Notice of Resumption of Opening of Bids.
6. **The contract is awarded.** The Commission publishes a Notice of Award, then a Notice to Proceed, and a Contract Agreement or Purchase Order. See **Notices and awards**.

Where two biddings fail, the Commission may move to **negotiated procurement** — it has done so, for example, for its janitorial services contract — and publishes minutes of the negotiation conference.

A bidding may be cancelled. The Commission's Notice of Cancellation states the legal basis:

> "Notice is hereby issued CANCELLING the bidding of the following projects pursuant to Section 41 of the Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations of R.A. 9184."

## What a supplier should do

Because the Commission does not publish a general submission procedure, the instructions that apply to a particular procurement are inside that procurement's own documents.

1. **Check the current year's bidding page** for opportunities and their closing dates.
2. **Download the Request for Quotation or Invitation to Bid** linked from the listing. The submission address, deadline, eligibility requirements and contact person for that specific procurement are stated in it.
3. **Read the Philippine Bidding Documents, Bid Data Sheet and Terms of Reference** for the same reference number, and check whether a Supplemental Bid Bulletin has amended them.
4. **Watch for schedule notices.** Openings are sometimes postponed and later resumed; both are published as separate notices.
5. **If anything is unclear, contact the Procurement Office** at procurement@psc.gov.ph or local 143 before the closing date.

## Reading a PSC bid reference

Bid references follow the pattern **PSCBAC-<number>-<year>** — for example, PSCBAC-007-2026 or PSCBAC-011-2024. Supplemental bulletins are numbered against that reference, as in "Supplemental Bid Bulletin No. 1 (PSCBAC-007-2026)". Older records from 2019 use a different form, such as GS 1234-2019. Quote the reference in any correspondence about a procurement.

## The legal framework

- **Republic Act No. 9184**, the Government Procurement Reform Act, and its Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations
- **Philippine Bidding Documents for Infrastructure Projects, 5th Edition**, for works contracts

Both are published on the Commission's procurement pages.

---

## What the PSC has not published

- **No BAC contact details, submission address or office hours.** They exist only inside individual bid documents.
- **No eligibility or accreditation procedure.** The invitation addresses "eligible & accredited" suppliers but the Commission publishes no process for becoming one.
- **No fee for bid documents**, where one applies, and no venue for the submission or opening of bids.
- **No supplier registry or list of accredited suppliers.**

If you need any of these in writing, ask the Procurement Office, or file a request through the **Freedom of Information** programme.
