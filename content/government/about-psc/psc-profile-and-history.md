# PSC profile and history

The Philippine Sports Commission is the national government agency that sets sports policy for the country. It was created by Republic Act No. 6847 on 24 January 1990, but the government bodies it replaced go back to 1911. This page traces that line, from the Philippine Amateur Athletic Federation to the Commission that runs the Rizal Memorial Sports Complex today.

## In retrospect: the early government sports bodies

The first known government sports organisation was the **Philippine Amateur Athletic Federation (PAAF)**, established in 1911 when the country was still under the American administration. The Federation's own handbook describes the spirit behind it as resolving the then-ongoing "menace of professionalism" and erosion in Philippine sports. Its first set of officers was all-American, including U.S. Governor-General W. Cameron Forbes as its president.

As amateur sports spread and international competition changed through the 1960s, the **Association for the Advancement of Amateur Athletic (AAAA)** emerged — described in the PSC's own account as a "kinetic and progressive" group of young sports leaders who believed that change in the PAAF system was unavoidable.

The historic meeting of 18 November 1960 brought AAAA representatives, leaders of different sports, the press and PAAF advocates together to map out that change. It led to a composite bill passed by Congress and approved by the President, and to a new PAAF Charter that decentralised the Federation's activities and gave the National Sports Associations their autonomy — a principle that survives today in Section 13 of RA 6847.

## Project: Gintong Alay

Presidential Decree No. 604 replaced the PAAF with the Department of Youth and Sports Development in 1974, which later became the Ministry of Youth and Sports Development. On 31 October 1979, Letter of Instructions No. 955 directed the Ministry to launch **Project: Gintong Alay** — the effort the PSC calls a ground-breaking moment in Philippine sports history.

The project's first goal was to integrate and intensify every athletics (track and field) development programme in the country. It was headed by **Michael Keon**. Gintong Alay introduced the training-camp concept to Southeast Asia, based in Baguio City: track and field athletes were the first group put through sustained, focused training aimed at international performance, and other sports were added later.

Among the athletes the PSC names as notable products of the programme are **Lydia de Vega, Isidro del Prado, Hector Begeo and Elma Muros**.

When RA 6847 took effect, the Gintong Alay Foundation, Project: Gintong Alay and all other government-funded sports foundations and associations were abolished, and their functions, appropriations, records, equipment and property transferred to the Commission (Sec. 25).

## The Commission since 1990

By 1990 the international sporting world was changing quickly and the national sports structure needed strengthening. President Corazon C. Aquino signed into law the creation of a new government sports agency with, in the PSC's words, "fortified and more relevant objectives". On 24 January 1990, the Philippine Sports Commission came into being as the top government sports body.

RA 6847 also transferred to the Commission the administration and full control of existing national-government sports facilities — naming in particular the **Rizal Memorial Sports Complex** and the **Quezon City Velodrome** (Sec. 23). The Commission today also operates the **Philsports Complex (Ultra)** in Pasig and a training facility in **Baguio**.

The **Philippine Sports Training Center** in Bagac, Bataan, institutionalised by Republic Act No. 11214, is the Commission's largest facility project. The PSC and the Provincial Government of Bataan signed a deed of donation covering six lots with a land area of roughly 250,000 square metres. The PSC has published no construction timeline or completion date for the centre; its page on the project has not been updated since September 2023.

## A note on the source text

The Commission's own profile page closes with a passage written for its 29th year and never revised — it still reads "For already twenty nine of productive service". The PSC marked its **36th anniversary in 2026**, a date that appears in its own 2026 procurement records. The history above is reproduced faithfully; the anniversary count on the original page is simply out of date.

The PSC publishes no separate "about the agency" narrative beyond this profile, and no message or biography from the Chairman: the Chairman's Corner page on psc.gov.ph contains a single photograph and no text.
